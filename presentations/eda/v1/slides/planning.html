What this means for planning

Volume is not workload

First-responder calls are 61% of incidents but 36% of vehicles. The forecast counts interventions, not the crews they need.

The riskiest days are shared

Storms hit several divisions at once, when units out of service hurt most. A count-only forecast will miss them; weather forecasts might (hypothesis).

Divisions differ

Division 6 grew while Divisions 1-5 fell. Splitting a citywide forecast by fixed shares would under-forecast it.

About 1 minute. What the findings mean for SIM's planners. One: we forecast volume, not workload. First-responder calls are most of the incidents but use few vehicles, so the forecast cannot tell planners how many crews they need. Two: the riskiest days are storm days that hit several divisions at once, which is exactly when taking units out of service is most costly. Our count-only design will not anticipate them; adding weather forecasts might, but that is a hypothesis and outside the agreed design. Three: Division 6 behaves differently, which justifies forecasting each division rather than splitting a citywide number.
